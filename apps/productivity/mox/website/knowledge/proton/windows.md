# Using windows on proton

Every app on proton opens in a window. To open an app, click the proton button at the left end of the panel and click the app in the proton menu, double-click its shortcut on the desktop, or click its button on the panel. Each app has one window: opening an app that is already open brings its window to the front instead of opening a second one.

## Parts of a window

A window's title bar shows the app's icon and name and three buttons: minimize (—), maximize (a square) and close (×). Below the title bar is the app itself. A grip in the bottom-right corner resizes the window. The window in front has a brighter title bar; windows behind it are dimmed.

## Moving, resizing, maximizing and closing windows

- **Move**: drag the window by its title bar.
- **Resize**: drag the grip in the bottom-right corner. A window is at least 300 by 200 pixels.
- **Maximize**: click the square button, or double-click the title bar; do it again to restore the window.
- **Minimize**: click the — button, or click the app's button on the panel while it is in front. Click the panel button again to bring the window back.
- **Close**: click ×. Some apps ask first if something is unsaved.
- **Bring to front**: click anywhere in the window, or click its button on the panel.

## Windows and the keyboard

The window in front takes the keyboard. Clicking a window, or opening an app, gives that app the keyboard, so app shortcuts such as Ctrl+S in the Text Editor or VLC's hotkeys just work. Clicking a text field keeps the cursor where you clicked. Games run in a frame inside their window: click the game once so it gets the keyboard.

## Windows on a phone or narrow screen

On a screen narrower than 760 pixels, windows open maximized, filling the space above the panel.

## Windows next to the AI pane

While the AI pane is open on a wide screen, windows make room for it: maximized windows end at the pane's left edge, and new windows open centered in the space that is left.

## When an app fails

If an app crashes, its window shows a card saying the app "hit a snag", with the error message, instead of breaking the desktop. Closing and reopening the app usually fixes it.
